## N-004 — The Exception-Operations Platform (autonomy that is earned, not asserted)

*ELI5: An AI worker that handles routine paperwork exceptions itself, earning more independence over time only as it proves it's getting them right — and losing it the moment it slips.*

- **Tier: GROUNDED LEAD.** v2: CORE, top-5 for 3/3 judges (CEO #1 at 87 — "the buyer story writes itself"); kill-pass REWORK folded in (never lead with the commodity matching happy-path; open on graduation/revocation). v3: survives re-grounding nearly as-is — it *introduces* the client's first agent and presupposes nothing. The strongest single concept under the corrected judge model.
- **DAY-1 STORY.** *Persona:* lockbox/cash-application exceptions specialist, WAB Alliance Association Bank operations. *Trigger:* the daily lockbox file lands; a tail of remittances fails auto-match — partial payments, missing coupons, one check covering three properties, stale account numbers. *Story:* today she researches each exception by hand (owner lookup, ledger check, call the management company). Day 1, the agent pre-works the whole queue supervised — proposed match + evidence chain per item, she approves or corrects; the graduation controller accumulates her verdicts per case-class and promotes routine classes to auto-post (Wilson-bound, n≥100), demotes automatically on drift. *Artifact:* daily reconciled cash-application report + exception dispositions with rationale + the graduation ledger. *Buyer:* head of Association Banking operations (a named WAB business line). *Dollar path:* $124.2B annual assessments across 373k associations; cash-app analogs run 95%+ touchless (HighRadius; Billtrust 45%→90% case) — per-bank P&L bottom-up estimate is an open T7 item, stated honestly. *Runs on:* the lockbox remittance file, association/owner master, dues-ledger export — files the bank already produces daily; file-in/file-out, no core-banking integration for the POC.
- **VISION ARC.** Five regulated exception queues (HOA dues, payroll garnishments, AML dispositions, dispute intake, cash-app breaks) are one problem shape; the durable IP is the **autonomy-graduation machinery** — supervised by default, promoted per case-class only on accumulated statistical evidence, demoted on drift. At scale: the outcome-priced operating layer under the mid-market back office; the graduation trust-ledger becomes the compliance spine (shared with N-001). This is also the sector's adoption story in miniature: every zero-agent bank's first agent should be hired this way.
- **Competition slice.** Unchanged from v2: HOA lockbox cash-application with visible autonomy graduation — 500 synthetic remittances, one case-worker agent + deterministic graduation controller, 95%-Wilson promotion trigger, CUSUM demotion on planted drift, seeded stream for reproducible promotion timing; one-page garnishment/ADP adapter proves the second queue on paper. Sizing: 2M, fits (smallest honest sizing among leads).
- **Demo moment.** Opens on the graduation ladder: supervised → evidence accumulates live → promotion of the routine class while ambiguous split-payments stay gated. Failure-recovery beat: planted drift trips CUSUM → automatic demotion on stage.
- **Freshness/applicability.** F2 with a strong demo mechanic; closest: Vantaca $1.25B (management-company side — category proof, not competitor), HighRadius (generic cash-app, no evidence-gated autonomy), Niural. Anchor WAB → ADP (garnishments), Visa/Capital One (dispute intake), Invesco (ops breaks). Subsumes R-004 (slice), R-005, R-006, R-008, R-023, R-030.
- **SaaS-judge checklist.** *User story:* exceptions clerk opens a pre-worked queue, approves with one click, watches routine classes graduate. *Runs on:* daily lockbox files + ledger exports (exist today). *Infrastructure:* one container + LLM API + a file drop; no agent platform prerequisite. *Pay?:* WAB's own line of business, manual loop documented, bank-side AI white space verified — POC-priced per queue. *Scales:* per-queue policy packs on one graduation controller; queue #2 (garnishments) shown on paper. *Vision realism:* graduation machinery buildable now; cross-queue platform is the honest 2-3yr arc. *Evals:* seeded ground truth per remittance + planted drift; per-class accuracy, pass^k, promotion/demotion timing — the eval harness IS the product's control surface. *Guardrails:* supervised by default; promotion only on statistical evidence; dollar caps; no write-back pre-promotion. *Failure handling/logging:* every action carries an evidence chain; unmatched → human with the missing field named; demotion automatic. *Cost:* cents per exception, published. *Latency:* batch workload — non-critical, published anyway. *Dollar impact:* match-rate delta × exception volume × loaded cost/exception; analogs above, bank-side number = T7.

## N-008 — The Remediation & Redress Worker (population-scale cleanup of any automated decision) — REFRAMED v3

*ELI5: An AI worker that, once a bank finds any of its automated systems got a decision wrong, finds everyone affected, recomputes what they were owed, and prepares the refunds and the regulator's notice.*

- **Tier: GROUNDED LEAD.** v2: CORE, top-5 for 3/3 judges (AI-judge #2 at 88, Eval-Grade A); strongest originality-provenance signal of the v2 run (two independent lenses converged on it). v3 reframe per the brief: from "agent remediation" to **remediation of any automated decision** — rules engines, models, fee policies, and eventually agents. Banks have been unwinding bad automated decisions for decades; the installed base is enormous and the felt need predates agents entirely (the Klarna/fee-waiver story works without a single agent in it).
- **DAY-1 STORY.** *Persona:* remediation/past-business-review programme manager, retail bank ops risk (Barclays-shaped). *Trigger:* internal audit finds a fee-waiver eligibility rule was misconfigured for a quarter — some vulnerable customers were charged fees policy says to waive. *Story:* today this is a months-long skilled-person-style exercise — analysts and spreadsheets enumerating who was affected, recomputing outcomes, drafting redress. The agent takes the confirmed defect signature → resolves the affected population from messy logs with an inclusion/exclusion proof per account → runs counterfactual re-decisions in parallel → deterministic redress calculator → drafts customer letters + the FCA notification → HITL release gate (autonomy capped at propose). *Artifact:* the remediation pack — cohort with proofs, exact redress schedule, drafted regulator notice. *Buyer:* head of remediation / ops risk; Consumer Duty makes closure non-optional. *Dollar path:* replaces months of Big-4 review time (s166-style engagements run £ millions); hard proxy = analyst-months → hours, exactness auditable. *Runs on:* core-banking transaction logs, fee schedules, policy documents, customer master — all exist at zero-agent banks.
- **VISION ARC.** Every assurance product stops at detection; the regulated, expensive work starts after. At scale this is the redress clearing layer of Consumer-Duty/CFPB finance — and when agents do arrive, the same engine closes *their* mistakes: N-001 finds → proves → N-008 repairs. Moat: auditable redress-math policies-as-code + the counterfactual re-execution engine + remediation precedent.
- **Grounding / why now.** Klarna's public reversal (May 2025) is the canonical drift precedent; 49% of UK adults show vulnerability characteristics; FCA Mills Review (2026-07-06) + new FCA debt-collection rules (2026-04-01); remediation is not optional under Consumer Duty. (`finance.md` §1, §3.)
- **Competition slice.** "The 140-account cleanup" — unchanged mechanics from v2, but the defective decisionmaker is explicitly a **rules engine, not an agent**: a fee-waiver policy bug ran 3 months, harming a seeded 140 of 1,000 accounts. Orchestrator → population resolver → parallel counterfactual re-decision workers → deterministic redress calculator → evidence writer → HITL release. Sizing: 2M, fits. Kill-pass rework folded in: the agentic center of gravity is the population resolver + counterfactual re-decisioning, never "the AI wrote the cover letter."
- **Demo moment.** Defect signature confirmed → cohort enumerated with 3 near-miss exclusions proven → per-account drill-down + drafted FCA notification. Failure-recovery beat: ~8 accounts have incomplete snapshots → engine refuses to auto-calculate and names the missing field. "A wrong redress number is worse than a slow one."
- **Freshness/applicability.** F1 — no agentic remediation product named anywhere in the briefs or the 2026-07-15 collision verification; closest: Big-4 manual past-business reviews. Anchor Barclays → Lendmark, Capital One, Amex; any regulated lender that has ever had to unwind an automated decision (all of them). Subsumes the repair verb missing from v1; completes R-047's detect→repair pair.
- **SaaS-judge checklist.** *User story:* remediation PM feeds the confirmed defect + policy docs; reviews the cohort proofs; releases refunds batch-wise. *Runs on:* transaction logs + fee schedules + policy docs (exist everywhere). *Infrastructure:* batch compute + LLM API; read-only access to exports; no agent platform. *Pay?:* remediation is a budgeted, regulator-forced cost center today — the rare AI sale with a deadline attached. *Scales:* new defect classes = new policy packs; same resolver/calculator spine. *Vision realism:* day-1 engine is buildable; agent-era closed loop is the arc. *Evals:* we authored the bug → we own ground truth; population precision/recall, redress exact-match, refusal-correctness on incomplete snapshots. *Guardrails:* propose-only autonomy; deterministic math outside the model; release gate is human. *Failure handling/logging:* per-account inclusion/exclusion proof; refuses on missing data and names the field. *Cost:* per-account pennies vs analyst-hours. *Latency:* batch — hours for a book, published. *Dollar impact:* Big-4 review displacement + error-free redress (over/under-payment risk both carry regulatory cost).
- **Appetite:** "months of remediation in an afternoon, every number provable" — a sales moment any bank ops-risk leader has personally bled for.
