### I11 · Regulatory-Reporting Reconciliation & Break Investigation — **4.35** `[Recommended]` `[Align with Zenon]`
- **One-liner.** Reconciles regulatory-return line items (Pillar 3 / COREP-FINREP / FR Y-9C-style) against the book of record, investigates each break with a root-cause hypothesis, and drafts a regulator-ready explanation memo.
- **Track.** A · **Source.** WS4-E + WS1-D
- **Zenon anchor.** Direct — Dow Jones GAAP-vs-management + Barclays portfolio + Visa DQ reconciliation, aimed at a regulated-reporting context.
- **Target user/client.** Regulatory-reporting / finance-risk reconciliation team at a bank filing Basel-style prudential returns.
- **The pain.** Return misstatements trigger fines and multi-year remediation; reconciling a return to the GL is manual, spreadsheet-heavy, and error-prone — Zenon's single most-proven pain pattern.
- **Why it's agentic.** Reconciliation agent matches return items to GL sources → break-investigation agent forms and tests hypotheses → narrative agent drafts an auditor/regulator-ready explanation; grows a rule library from resolved breaks (memory).
- **Synthetic-data viability.** Very high — trivial to fabricate a synthetic return template + GL with injected breaks (the I3 error-injection recipe).
- **6-wk feasibility.** High — I3's proven tech applied to one return template. AWS + capped LLM API.
- **Market/competition.** Incumbent reg-reporting vendors (AxiomSL/Adenza, Wolters Kluwer OneSumX, Vermeg — market share *unverified*) dominate report *production*; their break-reconciliation is rules-based, not explainable/agentic.
- **White-space/originality.** Moderate — crowded incumbent category, but the explainable-break-narrative angle is a genuine gap. **ZI 5** (proven Zenon DNA + fine-avoidance $), **TD 4** (solid multi-agent, but a known reconciliation pattern), **FR 5** (I3 tech, trivial synthetic data).
- **Sources.** Zenon project history (DJ/Barclays/Visa reconciliation); [LexisNexis true cost of compliance, Feb 2024](https://risk.lexisnexis.com/about-us/press-room/press-release/20240221-true-cost-of-compliance-us-ca)

### I12 · Payments & Settlement Reconciliation + Dispute/Break Investigation — **4.25** `[Recommended]` `[Align with Zenon]`

- **Track.** A · **Source.** WS1-F + WS1-J + WS2-E
- **Zenon anchor.** Visa cross-client reconciliation and data-quality discipline; payments-ops scope.
- **Target user/client.** Head of payments ops / dispute management / treasury ops at a PSP, acquirer, or correspondent/regional bank.

### I13 · Forecast Assurance / Variance Sentinel — **4.25** `[Recommended]` `[Align with Zenon]`

### I14 · Agentic-AI Model-Risk & Assurance — "MRM-for-Agents" — **4.25** `[Recommended]` `[Align with Zenon]`
- **One-liner.** A vendor-agnostic supervisor-agent that ingests a bank's *other* agents' action/tool-call logs, runs a battery of governance/adversarial tests (scope creep, unauthorized data access, missing human checkpoints) modeled on FINRA's risk vectors and the SR 26-2 gap, and drafts an exam-ready, cited violation dossier.
- **Track.** A · **Source.** WS5-A + WS1-E + WS5-H
- **Zenon anchor.** Barclays rules+ML explainability ("Apollo vs XGBoost"); cross-client validation/QC discipline.
- **Target user/client.** Model-risk-management / internal-audit lead at a mid-size or regional bank rolling out its first production agents (often 2+ vendor tools).
- **The pain.** The first Fed/OCC/FDIC MRM rewrite in 15 years (SR 26-2, Apr 2026) explicitly **excludes** generative/agentic AI from scope while requiring banks to govern it anyway; FINRA (Dec 2025) names agent auditability a top-4 supervisory risk — banks must self-govern with no template, manually.
- **Why it's agentic.** Meta-agentic — an agent reads other agents' traces, plans tests, executes them, cross-checks a policy library, flags drift/bias, and auto-drafts a validation memo with severity ranking.
- **Synthetic-data viability.** High — synthetic agent decision logs + a synthetic policy corpus with injected violations.
- **6-wk feasibility.** Medium-high — scope to one target agent type + one ruleset; output a scorecard + narrative.
- **Market/competition.** Enterprise AI-governance vendors (Credo AI, Patronus AI, Galileo, TrustLogix) exist but are not banking-specific with a rules+ML explainability lens; **none audits *other vendors'* agents** in finance.
- **White-space/originality.** Highest-timeliness finding in the sweep — the regulatory vacuum is brand new (Apr 2026). **ZI 4** (banks budgeting for it — Wells Fargo hire — though no named Zenon MRM client), **TD 5** (genuinely novel meta-agent), **FR 4**.
- **Sources.** [Federal Reserve SR 26-2, Apr 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/SR2602.pdf); [FINRA 2026 Oversight Report, Dec 2025](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/guidance/reports/2026-finra-annual-regulatory-oversight-report/gen-ai); [Sullivan & Cromwell memo, Apr 2026](https://www.sullcrom.com/insights/memo/2026/April/OCC-Fed-FDIC-Issue-Revised-Guidance-Model-Risk-Management)

### I19 · Cross-Jurisdiction Obligation Diff Engine — **4.00** `[Align with Zenon]`
- **One-liner.** Ingests the same regulatory topic (e.g., ICT-incident reporting timelines, AML thresholds) from three jurisdictions' public texts and produces a structured, cited, obligation-by-obligation diff.
- **Track.** A · **Source.** WS4-A
- **Zenon anchor.** Structural edge — validation/QC discipline and "does source A match source B, and why not" reconciliation mindset, applied to regulatory text.
- **Target user/client.** Head of regulatory change / compliance at a multi-jurisdiction retail or commercial bank.
- **The pain.** The same obligation reads differently across DORA, US OCC guidance, and RBI circulars; today the diff is built once per topic by outside counsel and goes stale immediately.
- **Why it's agentic.** Ingestion agent → obligation-extraction (NER + deontic classification, per ComplianceNLP) → cross-jurisdiction semantic-matching → report agent flagging low-confidence matches; persistent obligation knowledge base (memory).
