## Working notes (reasoning before the cards)

What do I actually see on the floor, direction by direction?

**Direction A.** The single most common dead-end phrase on my floor is some version of "I'm sorry, we can't give financial advice — you may wish to speak to an independent financial adviser." We say it dozens of times a day, and we all know the customer on the other end will never see an adviser, because advisers don't want customers with four figures to their name. So the customer hangs up, guesses, and often guesses wrong — and six months later they're back in my queue, angrier and poorer. The pain points I hear daily: (1) the advice wall itself — we know the answer but aren't allowed to say it; (2) hardship customers who don't know what help exists (benefits, tariffs, grants); (3) customers parked in visibly wrong products for years; (4) irreversible decisions made live on a call with nobody qualified in the room; (5) life events (death, divorce, redundancy) where the customer must make fifteen decisions while barely functioning; (6) the total absence of anything like ongoing coaching for ordinary-balance customers.

**Direction B.** From my chair, the tragedy of collections is that the bank almost always heard the warning first — literally heard it, on a call — and did nothing, because the person who heard it was a rep on a 6-minute handle-time target with no button to press. Pain points: (1) hardship signals dropped mid-call about unrelated matters, lost forever; (2) collections reps with three blunt tools and a script, offering forbearance that predictably fails; (3) payment arrangements that break silently; (4) small-business deterioration visible in transaction data months before the overdraft breach, but nobody's watching; (5) local-economy shocks (an employer closes) that hit hundreds of our customers at once and get handled as unrelated one-offs months later; (6) the pre-repossession escalations that land on my desk where I'm certain a better option existed three months earlier.

Design stance for every card: the agent must ACT — investigate, decide, produce an artifact, execute a step — because a chatbot that explains things is just my IVR with better grammar. And the "newly possible" test I apply: could this have been staffed by humans? If yes, someone would have done it; the interesting ideas are the ones that were always desirable but never staffable at per-customer, per-account granularity.

Balance: 6 cards in Direction A (G6-1..G6-6), 6 in Direction B (G6-7..G6-12).

## Direction A — Financial guidance for the unadvised

### G6-1 · After-Call Answer Pack
**Direction:** A
**The problem:** I coach reps to say "we can't advise you" cleanly, and I hate it every time. A customer asks "should I overpay the loan or build savings first?" — a question with a knowable, personal answer — and we legally shrug. Human advice doesn't reach these people: advisers typically won't take clients below wealth thresholds in the high five or six figures (UNVERIFIED), and the "advice gap" affecting millions of mass-market customers is a long-documented regulatory concern (UNVERIFIED).
**Who has it:** every servicing rep who has to deliver the shrug, and every mass-market customer who hears it.
**What the agent does:** After (or during) the call, it ingests the transcript plus the customer's actual account data, builds a personalised guidance pack — the customer's real numbers, the 2–3 realistic options, trade-offs quantified, eligibility pre-checked — runs it through a compliance rules layer that keeps it inside the permitted guidance/targeted-support boundary, files the suitability-style record, sends the pack, schedules a follow-up, and executes any product step the customer then confirms (opens the savings pot, sets the overpayment). The rep's job becomes "I can't advise you on this call, but you'll have a personal answer pack in an hour" — which is a sentence I could actually be proud of.
**Why newly possible now:** LLMs can now produce judgment-laden, personalised explanation grounded in a specific customer's numbers, with every statement checkable against a rules layer; regulators (e.g., the UK FCA's "targeted support" reforms, consulted on around 2024–2025, UNVERIFIED) are deliberately opening a lane between generic information and full regulated advice; and the marginal cost per pack has collapsed from adviser-hours to pennies.

### G6-2 · Benefits Sweep-and-File
**Direction:** A
**The problem:** Customers in hardship tell me their whole situation on a call, and I can hear — because I've done this twenty years — that they're entitled to help they're not claiming: means-tested benefits, social broadband/energy tariffs, council schemes, charity grants. Unclaimed means-tested benefits in the UK alone have been estimated in the tens of billions of pounds per year (UNVERIFIED). We are not allowed, staffed, or trained to walk them through any of it.
**Who has it:** financially stressed customers with no welfare-rights adviser; the collections and vulnerable-customer teams who can only signpost.
**What the agent does:** With consent, it reads the customer's transaction history and stated circumstances, sweeps the full entitlement landscape (benefits, tariffs, grants, debt-relief schemes), determines likely eligibility with confidence levels, pre-fills the actual applications, submits them where digitally possible or produces ready-to-sign packs where not, tracks each claim to decision, chases lapsed ones, and then folds the awarded income back into a revised budget and (if in arrears) a revised payment arrangement. It turns "have you tried Citizens Advice?" (UNVERIFIED as to naming) into money arriving.
**Why newly possible now:** open-banking transaction data gives an honest income-and-expenditure picture without an hour-long interview; LLMs can read the sprawling, constantly-changing eligibility rules and map messy real lives onto them; agentic form-filling and application-tracking across dozens of portals was never staffable per-customer before.

### G6-3 · Wrong-Product Whistleblower
**Direction:** A
**The problem:** I take calls from pensioners with five figures sitting in a legacy savings account paying near-zero while the same bank sells a 4%+ account on its homepage (UNVERIFIED as typical spread), and from customers paying monthly packaged-account fees for travel insurance they're too old to be covered by. They find out by accident, usually from an angry relative, and the call lands on my floor as a complaint.
**Who has it:** long-tenure, low-engagement customers — disproportionately older and less digital; and the complaints team downstream.
**What the agent does:** It audits the entire book, customer by customer: detects product-holding mismatches against the customer's actual usage and eligibility, quantifies the personal cost ("you have paid £312 this year for benefits you cannot use" — figures from their own account), generates a plain-language switch letter with the concrete alternative, executes the switch on a simple reply or call-back, and logs the whole thing as a remediation record. It deliberately costs the bank margin — that is the point, and it's what duty-of-care regulation (e.g., UK Consumer Duty, in force since 2023, UNVERIFIED) says should happen anyway.
**Who blinks first:** the bank that runs this openly wins the trust war; everyone else gets it imposed by a regulator.
**Why newly possible now:** whole-book per-customer product-fit auditing needs judgment (usage patterns, eligibility, life stage), not just rules — that's newly automatable; regulatory pressure now punishes the old "hope they never notice" economics; generation of individually-grounded, compliant customer letters at millions-scale is a solved cost problem.

### G6-4 · Big-Decision Second Chair
**Direction:** A
**The problem:** The worst calls I listen back to are the ones where a customer makes an irreversible decision live — cashes out a pension early, closes a fixed-term product at a penalty, sends a life-changing sum to a "safe account" or an investment their new online friend recommended — and the rep processes it correctly and helplessly. Authorised-push-payment scam losses run to hundreds of millions of pounds a year in the UK alone (UNVERIFIED), and early pension encashment is a well-documented harm area (UNVERIFIED).
**Who has it:** the customer in the moment of the decision; the rep who executes it; me, when the family complains.
**What the agent does:** It sits on the live call (and in the app journey) as a second chair. When it detects a consequential, hard-to-reverse decision forming, it acts in real time: pulls the customer's actual position, generates a one-page plain-language consequences sheet ("here is what this costs you at 67") pushed to the rep's screen and the customer's phone mid-call, runs coercion/scam pattern checks against the conversation itself, and where risk is high it injects structured friction — offers a no-penalty 48-hour cool-off it is empowered to grant, books a guidance callback with the answer pack (G6-1) already prepared, or escalates to a human specialist with a complete case summary. It doesn't block the customer's right to decide; it makes sure the decision is made with eyes open and without a scammer's hand on the wheel.
**Why newly possible now:** real-time speech understanding is finally good enough to follow a natural conversation, not keywords; LLMs can detect intent, pressure, and third-party scripting in dialogue; and an agent can now assemble the personalised consequences artifact in seconds, which is the only timescale that matters mid-call.

### G6-5 · Life-Event Autopilot
**Direction:** A
**The problem:** A widow calls three weeks after the funeral. She has to notify a dozen institutions, retitle accounts, deal with probate, decide what to do with a pension lump sum, cancel and restart direct debits — fifteen consequential tasks while she can barely finish a sentence. We give her a bereavement leaflet and our deepest condolences. Divorce and redundancy are the same movie with different sets.
**Who has it:** anyone hit by a major life event without a solicitor or adviser on retainer — i.e., almost everyone; and the bereavement/vulnerability teams drowning in paperwork.
**What the agent does:** It takes the whole case. From the customer's actual holdings it builds the complete task graph with deadlines; executes the mechanical steps itself (institution notifications, account freezes and retitling, direct-debit migration, standing-order cleanup); drafts the probate and claims paperwork ready to sign; sequences the judgment-laden decisions (keep or move the lump sum? sell or keep the second car on finance?) so nothing irreversible happens in the fog weeks; and coaches through each decision with a personal options sheet, flagging deadlines (e.g., time-limited claim windows, UNVERIFIED as to specific limits) before they lapse. One case owner, infinite patience, never on hold.
**Why newly possible now:** the task was always a case-management-plus-judgment job that would need a paralegal per customer — unstaffable; agentic AI can now hold a months-long case, act across systems, and generate the decision artifacts; digital death-notification and inter-institution rails (UNVERIFIED as to coverage) give it actuators, not just advice.

### G6-6 · The Fiver-a-Month Adviser
**Direction:** A
**The problem:** Wealthy customers get a human who watches their money and acts. My customers get marketing emails. There is no ongoing "someone is watching your financial life" service at a mass-market price point, because a human doing it costs more per year than these customers' entire product margin.
**Who has it:** the entire unadvised mass market — steady income, modest balances, zero slack for mistakes.
**What the agent does:** A standing, subscribed money-guardian that doesn't wait to be asked. It continuously monitors the customer's full financial picture; moves cash to better rates within mandate; catches and renegotiates creeping bills and lapsing promotional rates; sweeps for the entitlements in G6-2; pre-files simple tax reclaims where applicable (UNVERIFIED as to jurisdiction mechanics); rehearses upcoming decisions with the customer before they're urgent ("your fix ends in 90 days — here are your three real options, I've pre-checked eligibility for each"); and produces an annual plain-language review artifact — what I did, what it earned you, what's next — that would pass a suitability-file sniff test. Priced like a streaming subscription, not a percentage of assets.
**Why newly possible now:** this is a big swing that was structurally impossible until (a) agents could act under a bounded mandate rather than just recommend, (b) open banking gave a full cross-institution view, and (c) per-customer cost of continuous judgment fell from adviser-salary to compute. The advice gap exists because of unit economics; the unit economics just changed.

## Direction B — Early warning and intervention across the credit and asset lifecycle

### G6-7 · The Ear on Every Call
**Direction:** B
**The problem:** Customers tell us they're in trouble long before the first missed payment — sideways, mid-call, about something else. "Since I lost my job—" while disputing a card fee. Bailiff letters mentioned in passing. A rep on a six-minute handle-time target has no button for that; the signal dies when the call ends. Then ninety days later the same customer surfaces in collections as a "surprise."
**Who has it:** me and every team lead who listens back to calls and finds the warning was right there; the collections team who inherit late what servicing heard early.
**What the agent does:** It listens to every interaction — voice, chat, secure message — and mines them for pre-delinquency signals: income loss, relationship breakdown, gambling references, other-creditor distress, tone shifts across repeat calls. When the picture crosses a threshold it acts: opens a pre-arrears case, cross-checks against transaction data (salary stopped? new payday-loan credits?), assembles a tailored options pack (payment holiday eligibility, restructure options, the G6-2 entitlement sweep), and initiates gentle proactive contact through the customer's preferred channel with a concrete offer — before the first payment is ever missed. Every intervention is logged with the evidence trail that triggered it.
**Why newly possible now:** speech-to-insight at 100%-of-calls scale used to mean sampling 1% for QA; now full-coverage transcription plus LLM comprehension of implication (not keywords) is cheap; and the intervention side — per-customer option assembly and outreach — is exactly the labour that made "proactive pre-arrears contact" a perpetual slideware promise.

### G6-8 · First-Call-Right Hardship Desk
**Direction:** B
**The problem:** My collections reps have three blunt tools and a script. The customer gets whichever generic forbearance fits the script path, it doesn't fit their actual life, and the arrangement fails — industry re-default rates on generic forbearance are notoriously high (UNVERIFIED). Then we do it again, one payment cycle later, with less trust.
**Who has it:** collections reps and their team leads; customers ground through repeated failed arrangements.
**What the agent does:** Before (or during) the hardship call, it drafts the income-and-expenditure statement automatically from transaction data instead of a humiliating 40-minute interview; simulates the full permutation space of relief structures — term extension, rate concession, payment holiday, split arrangement, interest freeze — against the customer's real affordability AND the bank's lifetime-loss position; pre-clears the best structure against credit policy; and hands the rep a single pre-approved offer they can say yes to on the first call. It then generates the confirmation letters, sets up the arrangement in the servicing system, and registers the arrangement with G6-9 for monitoring. One call, one offer that actually fits, done.
**Why newly possible now:** the permutation-and-affordability simulation per account was a credit analyst's afternoon, unaffordable per collections case; agents do it in seconds inside policy guardrails; open-banking I&E replaces self-reported budgets; and regulators increasingly require individually-tailored forbearance rather than script outcomes (UNVERIFIED).

### G6-9 · Promise-Keeper
**Direction:** B
**The problem:** We take a payment arrangement, mark the case handled, and find out it broke only when the next payment bounces. Nobody watches arrangements between payment dates — there's no headcount for it — so a fixable wobble (hours cut, rent up £80) becomes a formal broken promise, a worse credit file, and a harder conversation.
**Who has it:** collections operations; the customer whose plan was two weeks from failing fixably.
**What the agent does:** It monitors every live arrangement daily against actual account flows: is the salary still arriving, at the same level? Are the essential-spend patterns still consistent with the agreed budget? When it predicts breakage, it acts before the due date — recalculates a viable adjusted plan, contacts the customer with "we can see this month is tighter; here's a one-tap adjusted arrangement," executes the amendment within pre-set policy bounds, and records it as a maintained (not broken) arrangement. Arrears cure becomes a tended process instead of a coin flip.
**Why newly possible now:** per-arrangement daily monitoring across a whole collections book is pure unstaffable-granularity work; transaction-level early signals plus an agent empowered to re-solve and re-contract within guardrails turns detection into intervention in the same motion — the exact watch-then-repair combination that never existed.

### G6-10 · Main-Street Early Warning
**Direction:** B
**The problem:** Our small-business customers deteriorate in plain sight — in their own current account. Bounced supplier direct debits, the VAT payment that didn't go out (UNVERIFIED as to signal validity), receipts down three months running, the owner's personal account suddenly funding the business. The "relationship manager" covers hundreds of accounts (UNVERIFIED as typical load) and meets the problem at the overdraft breach, when the options are all bad.
**Who has it:** SME relationship managers and business-banking credit teams; the business owner who'd have taken help at month two but gets a demand letter at month seven.
**What the agent does:** It watches every SME account continuously and reads it the way a good bank manager used to when they had twenty clients instead of four hundred: detects covenant-style drift even where no formal covenants exist, distinguishes seasonality from decay, and when deterioration is real it builds the intervention — a briefing pack for the RM (what changed, when, why it matters), a pre-modelled menu of options (temporary limit, restructure, invoice-finance bridge) sized to the business's actual cash-flow rhythm, a drafted outreach message in plain business-owner language, and a booked call. For the credit team it simultaneously updates the exposure view so provisioning isn't a quarter behind reality.
**Why newly possible now:** the judgment that made this a human-only job — "is this seasonal, structural, or a one-off?" — is now automatable over transaction narratives; per-account continuous attention across a whole SME book was never staffable; and open-banking-era data access plus agentic drafting/booking closes the loop from detection to a conversation on the calendar.

### G6-11 · Postcode Shockwave
**Direction:** B
**The problem:** When the big local employer shuts a site, I see it in my queues within weeks — same town, same story, call after call — but the bank sees it as hundreds of unrelated single cases, months later, in collections. Nobody connects them until the loss report does.
**Who has it:** regional credit-risk teams; front-line teams flooded without warning; whole communities of customers treated as isolated defaults.
**What the agent does:** It watches payroll-inflow patterns at employer level across the book. When salary credits from one employer stop or shrink for a cluster of customers, it declares a community credit event and runs a coordinated play: identifies every affected customer (including ones one hop out — the café next to the plant); auto-enrolls them into pre-emptive hardship pathways with tailored options packs before missed payments; arms frontline teams with situation briefings and talking points ("if a caller mentions [employer], here's the offer"); generates the portfolio-level artifact for risk — concentration exposure, expected-loss scenarios, provisioning recommendation; and tracks the cohort through recovery so interventions adapt as the local labour market does. One shock, one orchestrated response, at both the human and portfolio level simultaneously.
**Why newly possible now:** the employer-level inference from payroll credits is a data pattern banks have always technically held and never operationally used; connecting a macro signal to hundreds of individually-tailored micro-interventions in days is a coordination task no staffing model could ever cover — it is precisely what an agent fleet does.

### G6-12 · Last-Door Case Agent
**Direction:** B
**The problem:** The escalations that end up on my desk are the pre-repossession and pre-charge-off cases, and the thing that keeps me up is how often I can see that a better door existed three months earlier — a restructure permutation nobody modelled, a government scheme nobody checked (UNVERIFIED as to scheme specifics), an assisted-sale option nobody raised. By the time the case gets real human attention, the options have burned down to one.
**Who has it:** the customer at the edge of losing their home or business; the specialist recovery teams who get cases too late; the bank, which loses more in a forced sale than in almost any alternative (UNVERIFIED as general pattern).
**What the agent does:** The moment an account enters the pre-recovery pipeline — ideally long before, fed by G6-7/G6-9/G6-10 — it assembles the complete case file and exhaustively enumerates every alternative to crystallizing the loss: all restructure permutations, all relevant support schemes with eligibility checked, assisted or managed sale, term-and-rate surgery, third-sector debt solutions. It models each path for both the customer's outcome and the bank's recovery, produces a best-alternative dossier for the human decision-maker, and executes the chosen path's paperwork end to end. And the part I'd fight for: no repossession proceeds without the agent's machine-audited certificate that every alternative was enumerated, modelled, offered, and declined or failed — a "nothing else was possible" record attached to every file.
**Why newly possible now:** exhaustive alternative enumeration and dual-sided modelling per distressed account was a workout specialist's week of work, so it was rationed to the biggest exposures; agents make it universal down to the smallest mortgage; and the auditability regulators and courts increasingly demand around repossession (UNVERIFIED) is exactly the artifact this agent produces as a by-product.
