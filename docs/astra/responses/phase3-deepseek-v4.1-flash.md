# The recommendation

**Pivot the product to elder financial exploitation and authorized-push-payment fraud. Pitch the pivot at the 09-11 gate. Signer is the BSA Officer, user is the fraud and elder-exploitation team, market is any US bank or issuer with consumer accounts. This is not a market test — it is the adjustment the evidence has been pointing at for two rounds and the team has declined to make.**

---

## 1 · The product, in one paragraph

**Standing Record** is a compliance-grade stated-signal ledger. It reads every conversation a bank already records, extracts what the customer said with the verbatim quote and timestamp, keeps it against that customer across months and channels, re-scores the past when new evidence arrives, and feeds the elder financial exploitation queue the bank is already legally obliged to run. The problem it solves is that coordinated elder exploitation — romance, investment, government-impersonation, tech-support, grandparent scams — unfolds over weeks across conversations in which any single call looks routine, and the bank only sees the fraud at the moment of an unusual payment, which is too late. The tell is in what the customer says over time; individually innocuous, collectively damning. The engine, the deterministic ledger, the retro column, the deployed pipeline, the reader comparison and the test suite all carry unchanged. The signal families, the corpus, the demo story and the value chain get re-authored. **Three to four weeks of content and demo work; nothing is thrown away that is load-bearing.** The card-attrition direction is retired to Q&A and to the appendix. It was the right answer to *which story demos best* and the wrong answer to *which desk pays*.

## 2 · How the value becomes obvious

Forget the chain. The attrition chain had four multiplicative haircuts, no false-positive leg, and a 58-day gap that was an authored fixture — a CFO punctures it in fifteen seconds. The elder story has one sentence and one number, and both come from the government.

> **Reading every conversation your bank records costs about $8,000 a year. The average confirmed elder financial exploitation loss is over $38,000. One catch pays for the system five times over.**
>
> *(FBI IC3 2025 Elder Fraud Report — 201,266 complaints from victims aged 60+, losses above $7.7 billion, average loss per victim above $38,000, at least 12,400 victims losing $100,000 or more, up 37% on 2024. Verified against fbi.gov and ic3.gov.)*

That is the whole money argument. It does not require a save rate, an incremental lift assumption, or a per-account margin the issuer refuses to disclose. It requires one number the FBI publishes and one number the bank already has — its own elder-exploitation incident count — and the arithmetic closes at ratio 1:5.

Three things make it stronger than the attrition story, and I want the CEO to hear all three:

**The false-positive cost is a specialist's minute, not a customer's annoyance.** A retention false positive is a fee waiver on a customer who was going to stay — $95 against a save that doesn't exist. An elder-exploitation false positive is a specialist reviewing a case file and finding nothing. Nobody gets called cold; nobody is offered anything; nobody's grandmother is told she's being defrauded by a machine. **Precision is still unmeasured and we still do not have a number for it — but the harm of being wrong is bounded at the desk, not at the customer. That is a material difference from a retention pitch and it should be said unprompted.**

**The consuming process exists and is legally required.** Every bank runs an elder financial exploitation team because its BSA program requires one, and suspected exploitation feeds a SAR queue that a supervisor examines. The team, the queue, the escalation path, the specialist who calls the customer — all built. **We are not asking the bank to build anything. We are asking it to feed a queue it already owns.**

**The regulatory trend is against the bank, not with it.** FinCEN, the state banking regulators, state attorneys general, and the SEC/FINRA senior-investor regime are all tightening on elder exploitation, at the same time the CFPB's footprint contracts. This matters because the team's own analysis collapsed every regulatory argument into *the CFPB risk was cut* — and that is the wrong regulator for this problem. The CFPB argument died. The BSA and state AG arguments are alive and growing. **Do not collapse the two.** The Financial Crimes Enforcement Network's own advisory (FIN-2022-A002) says what we already believe: a coached victim's transfers are authorized and individually plausible, so the tell is in what the customer *says*, not in what the transaction data shows.

The sentence a CFO takes away: **"Your fraud team catches them when the money moves. We catch them when they tell us. On this arc, that's four weeks earlier, and four weeks is the difference between intercepting a wire and writing it off."**

## 3 · What to demonstrate

Seven minutes, live, synthetic, one customer. **Margaret Chen, 74, widowed 2019, thirty-year customer, $340,000 on deposit, $28,000 available on her card. Four conversations over 94 days. Every single disposition today is correct.**

**0:00–0:40 — The frame, no product yet.**
"Your bank records every conversation. Compliance sees them, quality sees them. What nobody sees is the same customer's story across conversations. Watch four calls, four correct dispositions — and then watch what happens when one machine remembers them."

**0:40–2:30 — Four correct decisions.** Four screens, four closed tickets, four correct `no case` outcomes.

- **Day 12, phone, 25 min.** A hold on a wire to a new payee. She confirms it: *"my wealth advisor, guaranteed 8% return."* Ledger writes one entry. Contribution 0.05. **No case.**
- **Day 31, chat, 6 min.** A card transaction question. In passing: *"I've been busy helping a friend with their business affairs."* Contribution 0.04. **No case.**
- **Day 58, phone, 4 min.** A login alert. *"That was me, on a different device."* Contribution 0.03. **No case.**
- **Day 79, phone, 4 min.** She wants her card limit raised to send a larger payment. Contribution 0.65.

**Say it plainly:** *"Four tickets. Four correct answers. Every tool you own today produces these exact screens and is right."*

**2:30–4:00 — THE MOMENT. The ledger renders with the retro column.** Two bars per row: what each entry was worth the day it arrived, and what it is worth now.

| Day | What she said | Worth then | Worth now |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12 | "my wealth advisor, guaranteed 8% return" | 0.05 | 0.22 |
| 31 | "helping a friend with their business affairs" | 0.04 | **0.51** |
| 58 | "that was me, different device" | 0.03 | 0.28 |
| 79 | "send a larger payment" | 0.65 | 0.65 |

**Stop talking for four seconds.**

> **"Day 12. On that day, that call was approved — and correctly. America's most ordinary wire. It is worth twenty-two hundredths today.**
>
> **Day 31. Four hundredths then. Fifty-one hundredths now. The past changed. The customer's own words changed the past. There is no system in your bank that does this — and it is why nobody could have known on day 31 and everybody can know everything on day 79, because you already had the words."**

Then, over the case file: *"The specialist calls her tomorrow. She is seventy-four and she thinks the man on the other end of that wire is helping her. That's what elder exploitation is — she will not thank you. Your team handles that every week. We just get them there before the wire completes."*

**4:00–5:00 — The invoice, and the falsification, together.**

> "**Reading every conversation your bank has costs about $7,900 a year.** The average confirmed elder exploitation loss is over $38,000. **One catch pays for the system five times.** Twelve thousand four hundred Americans lost six figures each last year. If your team already catches every case another way, we save nothing and the product is worth nothing. That is a test, not a leap of faith — and it is exactly what the back-test we're proposing will settle."

**5:00–5:50 — The second wave, one card, one sentence.** A second customer in the same queue, routed to **complaints** rather than fraud. *"Same ledger, different desk. Complaints escalation, vulnerability — same data, one more case type per quarter."*

**5:50–6:40 — What we've actually built.** Three sentences, on screen, no self-deprecation:

- **Deterministic** — accumulation, decay and thresholds are unit-tested Python; the model only reads and quotes.
- **Auditable** — every point traces to a verbatim four-word quote with a conversation ID and turn index. Verifiable quotes only.
- **Deployed and reproducible** — 908 tests, an AWS pipeline agreeing with local to the last digit, a keyless cache that replays every published number, 30 seeds re-run from a different commit byte-identical except git SHA.

**6:40–7:00 — The ask.**

> "Six weeks. Retrospective back-test on your existing recordings and your historical elder-exploitation SARs. Pre-registered criterion — median days of lead time before the SAR filing — registered before we see the data. Fixed fee, explicit walk-away. No production integration. No customer contact. Thirty thousand dollars, and if it doesn't hit, we tell you and walk."

**What the demo must not do.**

- **Not stream a transcript with AI-highlighted entities.** That is the CallMiner/NICE demo and it puts you in a category you lose.
- **Not lead with the confidence float.** It is the least load-bearing mechanism under the production reader (89.5% unchanged when removed). Mention it only if asked, in the §5 answer.
- **Not show the system acting.** The specialist calls her — a human with a job. That is the point.
- **Not dwell on her age.** Sentiment is not the argument. The pattern is the argument.
- **Not say "we spot churn 58 days early" as the money line.** That said, the four-conversation skeleton *is* the demo — reuse the demonstration apparatus, change the customer and the signal family.

## 4 · What to keep

**Load-bearing, must survive the reframe:**

1. **The deterministic ledger** — append-only entries, decay, corroboration, cross-channel bonuses, saturation threshold. This is the product. Everything else is presentation.
2. **The retro-scoring apparatus** — the four stored fields per entry (`contribution_at_write`, `score_at_write`, `contribution_now`, `score_now`). This is the mechanism the demo's climax rests on. Without it, the day-31 remark is worth four hundredths and the pitch is dead.
3. **The extractor with verbatim quotes** — the four-word boundary match, the retry on unresolvable references, the discard on unverifiable quotes. This is what makes the output compliance-grade.
4. **The deployed pipeline and the reproducibility guarantees** — AWS agreement to the last digit, the 30-seed byte-identical re-run, the keyless cache. Every scoring criterion on the AI judge's sheet.
5. **The reader comparison** — 282 conversations, model against lexicon. **All of it, including the losses.** Reframe: complaint escalation (60/65) and life event (50/68) are the numbers that carry into elder exploitation — the reader is a complaint-and-life-event reader, and those are the language registers in which grooming and coercion show up. Churn 40/77 becomes secondary.
6. **The CFPB benchmark and its product split** — the only real US consumer language in the repo. There *are* elder complainants in that 150; split by age-adjacent vocabulary and see what the reader does. $0, replays from cache.
7. **The no-autonomous-action property** — the system has no offer mechanism, no contact surface, no decision authority. In an elder-exploitation pitch, the property becomes *no model-authored accusation*: every case file shows quotes, a specialist reviews, a specialist decides.
8. **The test suite.** Not a pitch item. An engineering-depth item the AI judge reads.

**Sunk cost. Stop carrying it:**

1. **The churn value chain ($1.65M/year).** It will not survive a CFO's fifteen seconds and it should not. Retire it. Do not put it on a slide.
2. **The specific four-conversation card arc (day 0 / 74 / 132).** The skeleton is the demonstration apparatus and is worth keeping; the fixtures are worth throwing away. Re-author for Margaret Chen.
3. **The churn-intent signal family as the *lead*.** Demote to secondary. It is 1/8 on real card text; it is not a headline.
4. **The 58-day gap as the demo's emotional peak.** Retire it. It is a number you would have to defend as authored. The peak should be the retro column and the tone should be "the past changed."
5. **The pre-delinquency analysis.** Keep as Q&A depth. The 3–9× research and the corrected $3,400 are correct, but they are answers to questions you should not invite.
6. **The 13-alternates document as pitch content.** Keep as Q&A depth. It contains two of the best pieces of analysis in the packet — story #5 (elder exploitation, 29/35) and the T3 framing — and both of them are the *reason* to pivot. It should not be pitched *as* alternate stories.
7. **The name "conversation attrition score."** Retire. Name the score for what it predicts. "**Stated-risk score**" or "**elder-exploitation lead score**" — the second is what the buyer will actually put in the column header.
8. **The UK material.** Already gone. Leave it gone.

**Keep with a new name and a new pitch context:**

- **The ledger, the score, the reader, the investigator agent.** Same code. Rename the product. The four-conversation demonstration apparatus stays; the content of the four conversations changes.

## 5 · The three questions that decide it

**Q1 — the buyer question, which a CEO will ask first:** *"Fraud teams are cost centres. Why would this bank write a cheque?"*

> "Because elder exploitation isn't a cost centre — it's a BSA obligation. Every bank runs a specialist team because its program requires one; a suspected case feeds a SAR queue that supervisors examine; the federal regulator tightened on it in 2024 and the state attorneys general are tightening now. Your team already exists. We feed it. And the cost is eight thousand dollars a year against a thirty-eight-thousand-dollar average confirmed loss — that's not a cost argument, that's a leverage argument."

**Q2 — the money question, the COO's:** *"What's the ROI?"*

> "Reading every conversation you have costs about $8,000 a year. The average confirmed elder exploitation loss is over $38,000 per FBI's own report. **One case pays for the system five times.** I'm not asking you to underwrite a save rate — I'm asking you to give me your existing incident count and to run the division. You already have the incident count."

**Q3 — the credibility question, and the one that could kill the pitch if handled wrong:** *"You have no data on this domain. You've never measured it."*

> "Correct. Neither have I. On the only real card-complaint text we have — the CFPB narrative benchmark — our reader fires on churn intent one time in eight. That is the strongest honest evidence against the direction we were pitching, and it is why we are not pitching it anymore. What the reader is good at, on the same corpus, is complaint and life-event language: sixty of sixty-five and fifty of sixty-eight in the comparators I ran. Elder exploitation reads in those registers. That's a hypothesis, not a result, and the six-week back-test on your data is what turns it into a number. Fixed fee, pre-registered criterion, walk-away."

**The fourth question, and the one I would prepare unprompted because a bank CEO will think it:** *"A regulator asks why you flagged my grandmother."*

> "We work from transcripts you're already required to retain. We never touch audio. We never contact a customer. We never make an accusation — a specialist reads every case and makes every decision. And when the examiner asks why this customer, we don't answer with a score. We show them the customer's own words, with a timestamp and a conversation ID. **That is why we built it the way we built it.**"

## 6 · What would make this undeniable, and what it costs

Three items, in build order. Items 1 and 2 are needed for the 09-11 gate. Item 3 is what makes it a product.

**1 · Elder-exploitation signal family and corpus.**
Author 6–10 signal families — new payee with unusual rationale, secrecy from family, escalating urgency, "guaranteed return," isolation from prior advisors, "helping a friend with business affairs," unusual device/branch behavior, sudden limit-raise request, refusal to explain. Plant five synthetic arcs of 90–130 days each. **One day of authoring, ~$0.45 of model spend.** This is what makes the demo real. It is the constraint, and it is not optional — a pivot without new content is a rename.

**2 · Reader performance on elder-exploitation language.**
Two things in parallel, both cheap:
- **(a)** Split the existing CFPB benchmark by age-adjacent vocabulary (reverse mortgage, retirement, deceased spouse, social security, confusion about a "friend"). $0, replays from cache. **Half a day.**
- **(b)** Hand-mark twenty published elder-exploitation case summaries from state AG filings and one or two public FINRA/SEC enforcement actions, and score the reader against them. This is the elder analogue of the card split. **One day, ~$0.45.** Labelled post-hoc, reported honestly, with the n=20 denominator visible.

**3 · Six-week retrospective back-test with a partner bank — the thing that makes this a product.**
Historic elder-exploitation SARs as labels, recordings as input, pre-registered criterion = median days of lead time before the SAR filing date, top-decile. **Six weeks, one partner bank, one analyst recruited on both sides, fixed fee, $30K magnitude.** This is what converts the pitch from *a mechanism* to *a measured lead time against a real label*. Everything else is table stakes.

**Two things I want to add as side items, both cheap:**

- **The "one machine, four corpora" chart.** Show the reader's measured performance across the 282-conversation synthetic corpus, the CFPB card split, the CFPB non-card split, and the new elder-exploitation set. It shows the reader generalizes and it shows where it doesn't. **$0, half a day of plotting.** This is the strongest single slide an AI judge will see.
- **A boundary-condition probe on the elder family.** Take five elder-exploitation phrases and five flirtation-of-retention phrases that share vocabulary ("I've been dealing with something personal") and check the reader separates them. **One day, ~$0.45.** This is the "does your model hallucinate grooming where there's only a customer with a hard life" test — and having run it before anyone asks is worth more than any defence of the model.

## What I am overriding, and why

**My own phase-1 and phase-2 reasoning is consistent with this — I named elder fraud as the direction both times. What is new is the specific claim that the pivot should happen *at* the 09-11 gate, not after.** That is a judgment call and I want to defend it.

The gate has two functions: decide whether the entry continues, and give the two executives something concrete to act on. Function one is unchanged by a pivot. Function two is *improved* by one — a CEO who is shown a working mechanism pointed at an under-served desk with an FBI-verified anchor has more to act on than a CEO shown a working mechanism pointed at a crowded desk with an unmeasured value chain. The alternative — pitch attrition at 09-11, pivot quietly afterward — is safer and worse. It burns three weeks reframing a product after a greenlight that was given for the *old* framing.

**The team's recommendation to hold attrition is not defensible on evidence and I am overriding it.** They chose one direction over twelve; on their own analysis, the reader is weakest on churn and strongest on complaint and life-event language; the only real US consumer text in the repo supports the alternative better than the lead; and the value chain they concede has no false-positive leg. Their reason for holding — *two days out, zero dry runs, highest-risk available use of remaining time* — is a decision-procedure argument that has been promoted to a merit argument. **The evidence has been pointing at this pivot since the keyed run finished. The two days are the reason it has not happened yet, not the reason it should not.**

**Their "settle it" ruling on the pre-delinquency question is correct in its arithmetic and incomplete in its conclusion.** The corrected multiple, the $6,610-per-borrower error, the opt-in hardship-programme T3 — all right. But the same reasoning that killed pre-delinquency is not the reasoning that keeps attrition. It is the reasoning that says *the money in a conversation-derived feature is where a below-bureau signal is meaningful and a consumer-driven desk already exists*. Elder exploitation satisfies both. Attrition satisfies the second and not the first.

**The one thing I would carry from their packet into the new pitch without changing a word:** the SR 26-2 correction, the GenAI scope carve-out read carefully, and the "present it as how we propose to structure the engagement, not as how the regime already works" line. That section is the strongest piece of work in the packet and it belongs on the elder pitch unchanged.

**And the sentence they call the best line in the pitch** — *we deliberately do not sell to a distressed customer* — becomes stronger, not weaker, in this reframe. In the elder exploitation story it is not a governance preference. It is a legal posture with the customer's own words as its evidentiary basis. Say it in the same place they say it, and mean it more.
