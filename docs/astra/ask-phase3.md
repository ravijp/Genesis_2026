# Phase 3 — the recommendation

You have read the facts cold and formed your own view, then read the team's analysis and judged it.
Now decide.

## The situation

This goes in front of a **CEO and a COO**, both with finance backgrounds, who between them decide
whether this entry continues. **18 minutes including a live demo, then about 12 minutes of questions.**

What they have said they are judging: **how strong the product is · dollar value impact · could I pitch
this to a company and win a project.**

Formal scoring: impact 25 · technical depth 25 · feasibility and production readiness 25 · originality
15 · presentation 10. A separate AI judge scores engineering quality — evals, reproducibility,
accuracy/cost/latency evidence.

**Time is not the binding constraint.** If the right answer requires building something, re-pointing
the system at a different problem, authoring new content, or running a measurement, say so and say what
it costs. More time can be requested. A recommendation that is achievable but wrong is worse to me than
one that is right and needs three more weeks — this is not the final round.

## What I want

**1. The product idea, stated in one paragraph.** What should this be, pitched as what, to whom,
solving what. If that differs from the current direction, say so plainly and say what happens to the
work already done. If it does not differ, say that too — "keep it, here is why" is a real answer and I
will not read it as a failure to have an opinion.

**2. How the value becomes obvious.** This is the part I most need help with. Two finance executives
need to see the money without being walked through a chain of assumptions they can puncture. What is
the argument, the number, or the demonstration that makes value self-evident? If the honest answer is
that value cannot yet be made obvious, tell me what to say instead and why that is still fundable.

**3. What to demonstrate, and how.** Seven minutes of live demo. What should be on screen, in what
order, and what is the moment that has to land? If what should be shown does not currently exist, say
what to build and roughly what it takes.

**4. What to keep.** Explicitly. Substantial work exists — a deployed pipeline, a test suite, an
evaluation harness, a measured reader comparison, the accumulation and retro-scoring mechanism. **Tell
me which parts are load-bearing and must survive any reframing**, and which are sunk cost I should stop
carrying. I would rather not discover later that a reframe quietly threw away the strongest thing here.

**5. The three questions that decide it**, with the answers. Not a long objection list — the three that
actually determine whether these two people say yes, in their words, with what I should say back.

**6. What would make this undeniable**, and what it costs. If there is a measurement, a build, or a
piece of evidence that converts this from interesting to fundable, name it with a price and a duration.

## How to answer

- **Decide.** Options with trade-offs are not what I need now. Pick, and defend the pick.
- Where your recommendation overrides your own earlier reasoning or the team's, say which and why.
- **Be concrete enough to act on.** Words I can say, screens I can show, things I can build. Not
  principles.
- If your honest recommendation is that this should not be pitched in its current form at all, say that
  first and say what to do instead.
