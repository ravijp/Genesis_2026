Ear on Every Call

A conversation signal layer for banks.

A bank’s customers tell it what is about to go wrong. Today those warnings are read one conversation at a time and then set aside. We read every conversation, keep every signal in a permanent record for each customer, and re-read the older ones whenever new evidence arrives.

01
Read all of it

Every call, chat and written complaint is read. Banks review roughly one in fifty today, by hand, for coaching.

02
Throw none of it away

A remark too faint to act on today is still evidence tomorrow. Every other tool closes the ticket and moves on. We keep it.

03
Re-read the past

One running score per customer per concern. New evidence changes what the old conversations mean, so the score is recomputed over all of them.

01 · The problem

Every tool in a contact centre works inside a single conversation.

  • A retail bank handles millions of customer conversations a year across calls, chats and written complaints. Contact centres record and retain them as a matter of course.
  • Each conversation is assessed on its own and then closed. Whatever did not clear the bar is stored and never read again.
  • Journey analytics products do join a customer’s touchpoints, but they aggregate events and outcomes for reporting. None of them keeps the evidence that failed the bar as a live, per-customer record that can be re-read later.
  • So a customer drifting toward trouble over months looks like several unrelated events, each correctly closed. Nothing in the bank is holding the pattern, because no system owns the customer across conversations. The CRM holds facts and outcomes. The recordings sit in an archive nobody re-reads.

02 · What we built

Keep every signal. Re-read the past when the present changes what it meant.

1 · Read all of it

A model reads every conversation and pulls out signals of four kinds: money trouble, intent to leave, a life event, a complaint escalating. Each signal carries a confidence and the exact words it came from, so a reviewer can check it.

2 · Keep all of it

Every signal is written to that customer’s permanent record, including the weak ones that clear no bar. Nothing is ever deleted. This is the inversion: every other tool discards what did not meet the threshold, which is precisely the evidence needed later.

3 · Re-score the whole record

Each customer has one running score per concern. When a new conversation lands, the score is recomputed over everything ever recorded for them, not just the new part. Old evidence can become the thing that carries a case.

When the score crosses the line, an agent investigates. It pulls the account, the transactions and any prior cases, then writes a case file: what it thinks is happening, which team owns it, what it recommends, and every quote it relied on with the date and channel.
A person decides. Always. The system has no way to contact a customer. It cannot email, message or call one. That is not a setting that is switched off, it is a capability that was never built, so a bank’s risk function can verify it by inspection.
What the bank actually receives is not an alert. It is a finished case. The evidence is already assembled across months, quoted word for word and dated, and it lands in the queue of a team that already handles that customer.

03 · Where it sits

Between the conversations you already record and the teams you already run.

Only the reading is done by a model. The counting, the fading of old signals and the threshold are ordinary program code: the same inputs always give the same score, and a bank can step through the arithmetic line by line. The model never decides how much something is worth, only what was said.

04 · Scores and thresholds

A score is accumulated evidence. A threshold is how much your team can handle.

What the score is

One number per customer, per concern, between 0 and 1. It is the weight of everything that customer has ever said about that concern, adjusted four ways:

  • Age. Old evidence fades. Money trouble halves in weight after 200 days, a complaint after 90. A job loss two years ago is not live risk.
  • Repetition across conversations. Saying it twice on separate days counts for more than saying it twice in one call.
  • Repetition across channels. Saying it on a call and again in a chat counts for more than saying it twice on the phone.
  • Escalation. A pattern that is getting worse counts for more than a flat one.

What the threshold is, and who sets it

It is not a model parameter. It is a capacity decision the bank owns. A team says how many cases it can genuinely work, and that sets the line.

  • If a team can review the top 10% of the book, the threshold is wherever that cut falls. Give the team more capacity and the line drops, so more customers surface.
  • This is why coverage and workload are the same conversation. Every extra case surfaced is an extra case somebody has to work.
  • Two thresholds exist on purpose: a fixed cut in the live pipeline, and a capacity-derived cut when we measure, so results are comparable across methods.
Why re-scoring matters, in the reviewer’s terms. Because the case that lands is not “this call looked bad”. It arrives with months of evidence already gathered, each quote dated and attributed, and the record shows what every conversation was worth on the day it happened and what it supports now. That is what makes the decision defensible to a regulator, and what saves the reviewer from assembling the history by hand.

06 · How it is built

Three layers, and only the middle one uses a model.

Layer 1 · Deterministic core

No model, ever. Signal ledger, append-only, with no delete permission even in the database. The re-scorer, the four adjustments, the threshold.

Same inputs, same score, every time. Unit tested and reproducible.

Layer 2 · Agentic

The reader turns a conversation into signals with quotes. The investigator plans, calls tools for account, transactions and prior cases, then writes the case file.

Quotes are checked word for word against the transcript. Anything paraphrased is rejected.

Layer 3 · Human

A ranked queue per team. Approve, dismiss or route. A dismissal is written back so the ledger learns the case was not real.

Teams with no cases still appear, and cases the agent declined to route are shown rather than hidden.

07 · The data

The test data is synthetic, and that is a requirement of the experiment.

Why no real dataset can do this

  • We searched regulators, ombudsmen and the public dataset repositories. Nothing public has repeated contacts from the same identifiable customer over time in financial services with an outcome attached.
  • More fundamentally, to score this system we must know how much evidence was placed in each conversation before the words were written. No real dataset can supply that, because nobody labelled it in advance.
  • We rejected stitching real complaints into invented histories. It produces incoherent people and destroys the answer key that makes measurement possible.

How a customer is built

  • The answer key is written first. A planner fixes the trajectory, splits the evidence across the conversations, and schedules which contacts end in a promise and whether it is kept. Only then is any prose written around that plan. A model never writes the answer key, and the outcome is sampled from the planted evidence rather than assigned.
  • Every customer is one of five kinds, drawn on fixed weights, and the label comes from the generation parameters rather than from what any method can detect.
Diffuse — evidence spread thin across the arc30%

Diffuse is the customer this product exists for. The two decoy kinds test opposite failures: firing on a look-alike is a mistake, while firing on a real but harmless signal is correct and the question is whether we pile it up into a case that should not exist.

One thing here is real, and it is the reader benchmark. To check the reader against genuine customer language rather than our own prose, we use 150 complaint narratives published by the US consumer regulator: real writing by real people, public domain, scrubbed of personal data by the publisher. They were marked up blind before the reader was ever run, giving 112 cases it ought to find. The model reader finds 92 of 112. The keyword baseline finds 4 of 112.

Real transcripts set the shape of our writing. Not one real sentence ships inside our corpus.
Synthetic data is also a competition rule. Every rate in this deck is quoted with its denominator, and nothing is published from a single run. 9 / 14

What we deliberately will not invent

Two numbers turn 37,500 surfaced customers into pounds, and both belong to the bank:

  • The save rate. Every retention desk reports it monthly.
  • The value of a relationship. Held in their own management information, and for credit portfolios audited under IFRS 9.

That is why the first engagement is two weeks on data they already own, not a six-month build. A CEO who catches an invented return stops believing the measured part, and the measured part is the good part.

The real constraint is capacity: 37,500 surfaced customers is more than any retention team can work, so the deliverable is the ranking and the cut, not the list. 11 / 14

Appendix A

The scoring maths, in full.

Ordinary code, no model. Reproduced exactly on every run, and steppable line by line.

score  =  1 − e−k · base · multiplier   k = 0.35, which keeps the score readable between 0 and 1 and cannot change any ranking
base  =  sum over signals of  ( confidence × 2−days old / half life )
multiplier  =  ( 1 + 0.35 × extra conversations ) × ( 1 + 0.20 × extra channels ) × ( 1.25 if escalating )
Half-life by concern, in days
ConcernHalf-lifeReasoning
Complaint escalating90Anger cools fastest
Intent to leave120Decisions get made or abandoned
Money trouble200Persists across cycles
Life event300Bereavement, illness, separation last

Why each adjustment exists

  • Repetition inside one call does not count twice. The reader collapses a conversation to one signal per concern first, so a customer repeating themselves in one call cannot inflate their own score.
  • Independent conversations reinforce super-additively. Three separate days is stronger evidence than one long day, which is exactly what a per-call tool cannot see.
  • Every adjustment can be switched off individually, and we publish what happens when they are. With all four off, no past conversation can ever be worth more than when it was written, which is the whole product in one sentence.

We do not claim to rank better, or to warn sooner. Accumulation needs conversations to accumulate, so it fires later than a per-call detector. The claim is reach: we surface customers who have no alarm at all today.

Unproven, with the price of finding out

  • The model’s own threshold. Coverage numbers use a cut derived from the weaker reader, so treat them as a ceiling. Deriving the real one costs $13.96.
  • Everything outside the reader benchmark is synthetic. Real lead time, real baseline and real false-positive load need a bank’s archive. That is the two-week engagement.
  • The investigator is not yet the model on the deployed stage, which is why 8 of 9 deployed cases return insufficient evidence. Roughly $0.90 and a re-run closes it.
  • One integration is modelled rather than live.
Where the test data is still unrealistic, in our own words. No conversation in an arc is ever silent, so “diffuse” currently means a faint signal every time rather than gaps between signals, and the genuinely hard case is not in the corpus. Live conversations take only six lengths and nobody interrupts. Complaints are a third of the channel mix, inflated so the written channel is measurable.
The discipline that produced this list is the product. Ground truth is authored before any text. The reader is mechanically prevented from seeing the answer key and a test fails if that boundary moves. Nothing is published from a single run. Every rate carries its denominator, and a headline that died is still printed beside the one that replaced it.
